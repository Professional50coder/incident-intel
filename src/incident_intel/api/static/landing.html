What you get

A review queue, not a wall of scores.

Ranked by how likely they matter

Consecutive flagged frames become segments with start and end times. Each gets a confidence label and a reason you can read, and the list is ordered so you know what to watch first. Labels are a heuristic, not a probability.

One slider for the false-alarm budget

Instead of a threshold you cannot interpret, choose how much of a clip you are willing to review. The default flags at most about 5% of frames, and only those clearly above the clip's own normal level. Segments update instantly.

Export JSON, CSV or Markdown, print to PDF, or share a link. Every report lists the settings, the ranked segments, the uncertainty notes and a reviewer sign-off checklist. This one was exported from the bundled sample.

Honest limits

What it can and cannot do.

It is unsupervised: a small autoencoder learned what a normal walkway looks like and flags frames that look different.

On UCSD Ped2 (frame-level AUC, test set) it scores 0.674. That beats chance and sits well below the best published methods, so expect missed events and false alarms.

It was trained on one scene. On other cameras, use the clip-relative mode and treat flags as a review queue, not findings. It scores every 5th frame, so very short events can be missed.

Not a certified security product. Do not rely on it for safety-critical decisions.

FAQ

Questions people ask first.

Is my video stored?

No. The upload is scored and deleted. Only the file name and scores are kept so you can reopen the report. History is tied to a random id in your browser, and anyone with a report link can open that report. A free host's disk can reset when it restarts.

Which videos work best?

Fixed cameras with a mostly consistent scene. Moving cameras and big lighting changes look unusual to the model and will be flagged.

What do the two modes mean?

The default compares each moment with the rest of the same clip and flags only the strongest few. The Ped2 mode uses a fixed threshold tuned on one benchmark scene and is only meaningful there.

How were the numbers measured?

The protocol, and what was and was not re-verified, is written up in the README. Prior-art notes are in the docs.
