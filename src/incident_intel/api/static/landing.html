How it works

1

Pick or upload a clip

Start with a bundled sample, or drop in your own video (mp4, avi, mov, mkv or webm, up to 30 MB).

2

Review the ranked moments

See a timeline and a list ordered most-likely first, each with a confidence label. Click to jump the video there, then confirm it or mark a false alarm.

3

Tune and export

Use one slider to trade missed events against false alarms. Export JSON, CSV or Markdown, print to PDF, or share a report link.

A real example

This is the actual output for the bundled cart crossing sample, a synthetic clip in which a wide object crosses the walkway at about 0:09 to 0:11. The sample is made-up footage for demonstration, not real surveillance video.

The exported Markdown report begins like this:

# Incident report: cart_crossing.mp4

- Duration: 0:20.0 (48 sampled frames)
- Threshold mode: **budget** (effective score threshold 0.00151101)
- Flagged frames: 3; segments: 1

> **Warning:** Most frames of this clip score above the Ped2-calibrated threshold, which usually means a scene the model has never seen. Use the clip-relative mode and treat flags as the most unusual moments in this clip, not confirmed incidents.

## About this report

Unsupervised conv autoencoder trained on UCSD Ped2 (frame-level AUC 0.674 on the official test set). Scores rank how unusual a frame looks; they are not verdicts.

Who it is for

Site and facilities teams

With one fixed camera and hours of footage to skim, to decide where to look first.

Reviewers who need a record

Every report lists settings, flagged segments and a sign-off checklist.

Students and researchers

A reproducible baseline with its benchmark numbers and limits stated openly.

What it can and cannot do

  • It is unsupervised: a small autoencoder learned what a normal walkway looks like and flags frames that look different.
  • On its benchmark (UCSD Ped2 test set, frame-level AUC) it scores 0.674. That is better than chance and well below the best published methods, so expect missed events and false alarms.
  • It was trained on one scene. On other cameras, use the clip-relative mode and treat flags as a review queue, not as findings.
  • It cannot say what happened. A flag can be a person running, a vehicle, a lighting change or camera movement.
  • It scores every 5th frame of the video, so very short events can be missed.

Not a certified security product. Do not rely on it for safety-critical decisions.

FAQ

Is my video stored?

No. The upload is scored and deleted. Only the file name and the scores are kept so you can reopen the report. History is tied to a random id in your browser, and anyone with a report link can open that report. A free host's disk can reset when it restarts.

Which videos work best?

Fixed cameras with a mostly consistent scene. Moving cameras and big lighting changes look unusual to the model and will be flagged.

What do the modes mean?

The default compares each moment with the rest of the same clip and flags only the strongest few, so false alarms stay bounded. The Ped2 mode uses a fixed threshold tuned on one benchmark scene and is only meaningful there.

How were the numbers measured?

The benchmark, protocol and what was and was not re-verified are written up in the README. The market and prior-art notes are in the docs.
